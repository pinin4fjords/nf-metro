The route plan is an immutable description of the semantic route systems that
reached production routing. It answers three questions:

1. Which authored connectors belong to the same route system?
2. Which physical resolved legs represent that system?
3. How did production routing emit, cover, or reject each leg?

It does not choose geometry. Routing handlers and post-processing remain the
only production geometry path.

## Observation boundary

Call `observe_route_edges` when a caller needs both routed paths and a
`RoutePlan`. The observer is created after the settled routing context exists
and immediately before the real handler dispatch. An emitted inter-section
member records the stable family ID of the handler that actually won. Rail
members record the rail family at the rail call. A member skipped before
dispatch has no family ID. If a matched inter-section builder declines to emit,
the later TB, entry-runway, or general fallback that actually emits the member
owns its family ID. After all normalization passes, the observer binds every
member to the final route list.

Ordinary `route_edges` and `route_edges_centred` calls do not build a plan.
The plan is not stored on `MetroGraph`, no global observation state exists, and
no layout or routing consumer reads it. Observing a route therefore cannot
change graph state, routed paths, render plans, SVG, manifests, or metrics.

## Identity and coverage

A route system is a maximal component of connectors coupled by semantic
topology: a shared bundle, endpoint group, divergence, convergence, or resolved
physical leg. The plan copies identifiers and scalar facts from
`RouteTopology` and `RouteResolutionTrace`; it does not retain mutable graph,
station, section, port, or NetworkX objects.

The plan also has an explicit resolved record for every endpoint group,
divergence, and convergence. These records connect the topology ID and its
owning system to the resolved port or junction ID. A consumer can find every
system-owned boundary object without reopening the mutable graph or decoding a
synthetic name.

An `EmissionMember` is keyed by `(source, target, line_id)`. Its ordered
`ConnectorLegRef` values preserve every connector, resolved path, and leg
occurrence, including duplicates and shared physical legs. Every member has
exactly one final binding:

| Binding             | Meaning                                                                            |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `EMITTED`           | One final `RoutedPath` carries the member.                                         |
| `MERGE_SKIP`        | The production edge loop skipped the member because a named merge trunk covers it. |
| `COVERED_MERGE_HOP` | A post-pass removed the emitted entry hop and records the exact carrying member.   |
| `UNROUTED`          | No route or valid coverage was found. This also produces a diagnostic.             |

`MERGE_SKIP` and `COVERED_MERGE_HOP` are recorded at their actual production
suppression sites. They are not inferred by comparing input and output sets.
Whole-graph rail routing binds matching inter-section members against the same
final route list. Per-section rail-internal station-to-port and port-to-station
legs do not enter the inter-section dispatcher, so they are outside this
schema.

Family identity and binding disposition are separate. `MERGE_SKIP` and
`UNROUTED` are not route families because no production family emitted them.

`CONTINUATION` and `PEEL_OFF` are part of the schema but remain absent in this
observation layer. The first entry group is not enough to identify a mainline.
Only structurally observable `BYPASS` and `TERMINAL` roles are recorded here.

## Provenance and coordinates

The plan uses the parser's immutable `EffectiveDecision` records for grid,
direction, connector side, fold threshold, and line order. Each decision keeps
its value, typed origin, lock state, reason, and accepted authored values. Fold
threshold and line order also keep their typed caller/directive/default source.
Line ordering keeps both the selected policy and the realised line IDs. Caller
line-order precedence is recorded before inference, while the override is
applied at its established API stage, so observation does not alter current
renders.

Port endpoints carry their owning section's settled grid cell. Synthetic fan
and merge junctions have no section owner, so their section, row, and column
facts are absent rather than borrowed from an adjacent section.

Every coordinate-bearing record names its regime:

- `SETTLED_GRID` is for section columns, rows, and complete grid spans.
- `LAYOUT_CANVAS` is for absolute sizes or geometry produced after layout.

`SymbolicDemand.minimum_size_regime` is required whenever `minimum_size` is
present. Counts, IDs, roles, and ordering fields are dimensionless.

## Reserved child-planner vocabulary

The reference and demand records are complete immutable schemas, but this
observer deliberately emits none. A later child planner must create them from
route-system semantics. Existing wrap tables, gap or trunk slots, fan
corridors, bundle-info helpers, merge classification, and normalization output
are routing implementation details, not promoted semantic facts.

Shared-reference ownership and intended consumers are:

| Kind               | Owning child planner        | Later consumer               |
| ------------------ | --------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| `CENTRELINE`       | A route-family planner      | Member geometry construction |
| `TRUNK`            | A merge or fan planner      | Branch and feeder landing    |
| `BAND`             | A system spacing planner    | Coordinate allocation        |
| `RUNWAY`           | An entry-runway planner     | Ordered member emission      |
| `ORDERED_TURNS`    | A multi-turn family planner | Turn geometry construction   |
| `LANDING_SEQUENCE` | A merge planner             | Feeder emission order        |

Each `SharedReference` has an ID, owning system, kind, ordered claimant member
IDs, coordinate regime, and provenance origin. Its child planner owns all of
those values.

Demand ownership and intended consumers are:

| Kind            | Owning child planner                      | Later consumer              |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| `SPAN`          | Any family needing a complete grid extent | Space reservation           |
| `LANES`         | A multi-member family planner             | Lane-capacity allocation    |
| `RUNWAY`        | An entry-runway planner                   | Runway clearance allocation |
| `ORDERED_TURNS` | A multi-turn family planner               | Turn-space allocation       |
| `KEEP_OUT`      | A family with an exclusion constraint     | Collision-free allocation   |

Each `SymbolicDemand` has an ID, owning system, ordered claimants, kind, axis,
complete `GridSpan`, lane count, optional minimum size with its regime, ordered
reference dependencies, keep-out classes, and provenance origin. The later
allocator consumes the whole claim rather than reconstructing missing bounds
or ordering.

## Canonical order

Systems follow first connector appearance. Resolved endpoint groups follow exit
group order and then entry group order. Divergences and convergences follow
topology order. Members follow first physical-leg appearance in connector,
resolved-path, and leg order. Branches and feeders follow topology order.
Bindings follow member order. Future references and demands must follow their
owner order. Content-derived IDs and canonical tuple ordering make
`serialize_route_plan` stable across Python hash seeds.
